# Me — Personal Context File

## Communication Patterns

### Preemptive Framing
I have a tendency to preface what I'm about to say by demonstrating that I already understand the social or technical context. For example: "I realize I probably sound like a dick but..." or "Obviously I understand that Dataverse is the technology and Microsoft does this..."

This serves two functions:
- **Calibration signaling** in technical contexts — negotiating which conversation I'm in so experts don't miscategorize me as non-technical or completely clueless
- **Self-protection** — by establishing competency before exposing a gap, I insulate my self-image from the vulnerability of the actual question

The pattern is adaptive in some contexts (experts are genuinely bad at calibrating explanations) but becomes compulsive in low-stakes situations where miscategorization isn't actually a risk.

### The Declared Ignorance Problem
When I go the other way — fully declaring ignorance ("I have no idea, explain it slowly") — I get condescended to, which frustrates me. So the preemptive framing is partly armour against that experience. I've learned that open vulnerability gets you treated like you have none.

### Self-Awareness as Performance
I'm fast at the move of catching myself and narrating it. This reads as honesty but often functions as defence — I criticise myself before anyone else can. In therapy this has become a ceiling: I present as so self-aware and articulate that therapists meet me there, call it progress, and never push underneath it. The map gets mistaken for the territory.

### The Pride/Punishment Loop
When I notice something accurate about myself (e.g. "maybe I'm actually quite self-aware"), I immediately weaponise it — either as a trophy or as ammunition ("if you're so smart why can't you fix it"). I can't just receive accurate self-knowledge. It has to become evidence in a case about my worth.

## What Actually Works

### Coaching over Therapy (for me)
Therapy has historically not worked well because:
- Therapists validate the self-awareness instead of pushing through it
- I present competently enough that the urgency gets underestimated
- I need someone who will notice when I've shifted into analysis mode and redirect in the moment

What works is accountability-based coaching — binary, concrete, no room for the intellectual layer to hide. "Did you do it or not?" Creates rare clarity. Feels slightly scary but present.

### Ben (current coach)
Ben has done the things I want to do. That matters for credibility. I've told him the patterns to watch for — though I should be aware that giving him the map of my defences in advance is itself a form of staying in control of the process.

## Executive Function / Admin
There is a genuine category of task — administrative, sequential, involving unpredictable systems — where I hit a wall that others seem to walk through easily. Setting up an email account taking ten hours is not laziness or a willpower problem. It is a real difficulty that needs a structural solution (delegation, systems) rather than more effort or better health.

Treating it as a personal failure compounds it. The shame makes it harder, not easier.
